## Anti-Patterns Identified by Virach Sornlertlamvanich

Virach Sornlertlamvanich explicitly warns against several common pitfalls and ineffective approaches, particularly in the fields of AI development, language processing, and societal problem-solving. Avoiding these anti-patterns is crucial for achieving robust and sustainable solutions.

### Sole Reliance on 'Reduce, Reuse, Recycle' for Waste Management
The anti-pattern of exclusively depending on the 'reduce, reuse, recycle' framework for managing plastic waste, which is insufficient for tackling the problem at its source.

This approach treats symptoms rather than preventing the problem, making it less effective than strategies focused on complete elimination and systematic replacement.

> The model represents the deviation from the “reduce, reuse, recycle” framework, emphasizing prevention over treatment and introducing innovative technological solutions for plastic alternatives.
(sources: src_014)

### Assuming Data-Driven AI Possesses Sufficient Reasoning and Knowledge
The misconception that data-driven AI models inherently have adequate reasoning capabilities and knowledge depth for complex tasks.

This assumption overlooks the inherent limitations of data-driven AI, leading to "knowledge insufficiency" and hindering advanced functionalities like enhanced conversational understanding.

> Challenged by data-driven AI limitations in reasoning and knowledge depth, this work presents a novel approach for enhanced conversational understanding.
(sources: src_014)

### Passive Consumption of Technology Without Local Knowledge Creation
The anti-pattern of merely consuming technology (e.g., buying devices) without actively investing in creating one's own knowledge platforms and fostering generic understanding.

This leads to dependence on external solutions, hinders true innovation, and prevents self-sufficiency and progress by making one a perpetual consumer rather than a creator.

> if you don't prepare the knowledge a generic understanding and solving the problem so feedback you are preparing some more telecommunication lives for somebody to sell you the telephone typical set computer sets
(sources: src_010)

### Sole Reliance on Rule-Based Classifier Assignment in Complex Languages
The anti-pattern of exclusively using rule-based approaches for classifier assignment in languages like Thai, where classifier choices are complex, fluctuating, and lack exact rules.

This fails because many classifier types are "open" due to their meaning representation, making it impossible to register all possibilities with fixed rules, necessitating corpus-based methods.

> Basically, there is no exect rule for classifier selection.
(sources: src_028)

### Uncritical Belief in Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) as a Universal Solution
The anti-pattern of assuming Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) is a definitive or complete solution for all problems.

This overlooks the inherent limitations and specific applicability of RAG technology, suggesting a need for a more nuanced understanding of its capabilities.

> RAG is not the answer for sure.
(sources: src_006)

### Using Unformalized Probabilistic LR Models
The anti-pattern of employing probabilistic LR (Language Resource) models that lack a probabilistically well-founded formalization.

Such models can degrade parsing performance, indicating the importance of rigorous mathematical and statistical foundations in NLP model design.
(sources: src_002)
